Network structure
Press Run then Ignite to explore bistability and gamma oscillations.
Model. Each LIF neuron obeys τ_m dV/dt = −(V−E_L) + RI(t), V_θ=20, V_r=10, t_rp=2ms, τ_m=20ms. Sparse random in-degrees (C_E=80, C_I=55) maintain the asynchronous-irregular regime. Recurrent excitation is slow (NMDA-like, τ_SE) for up-state stability; fast (F component) for E–I gamma resonance (PING). Inhibition is instantaneous delta — each I spike delivers a step suppression that resets E-neuron timing coherently. External input is OU noise with σ/√2 amplitude so stationary membrane variance matches the σ²/2 assumed by the Ricciardi transfer function. Intraburst gamma: when the network enters the up-state, the E–I feedback loop resonates (period ≈ 2δ + I-suppression time ≈ 15–30ms → 35–65Hz). Spike-frequency adaptation and STD reduce the effective excitatory drive over time, lengthening the period and producing a downward frequency chirp visible in the spectrogram (CH3). The mean-field self-consistency (right panel) captures the time-averaged rate but not the oscillatory fine structure — demonstrating what the 1-D fixed-point description can and cannot predict.
